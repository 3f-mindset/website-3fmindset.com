## Journal Prompts for a Working Vision

Use these questions to turn the Crucible material into a working vision you can actually use: a clear sense of what matters, how you want to participate, what qualities you want to bring, and what to try next. The questions extend [the Crucible progression](01-comprehensive-workshop-plan-from-vision-to-weekly-practice.md) and [the worksheet guide](05-working-the-crucible-vision-strike-evidence-review-recalibration.md); they do not replace the worksheets.

Write in your own language. Specific memories, scenes, words, and examples are often more useful than polished statements. You can answer in one sitting or in stages, skip questions that do not fit, and return when your circumstances change. You do not need to resolve every tension before taking a first step. Domains are areas of life you choose to focus on; roles are ways you participate in them; Edges are qualities you bring to those roles. None is a score of your worth.

## 1. Begin with what matters now

Start with the season you are actually in—not the season you think you should be in.

- What has been taking up the most space in your attention lately? What deserves more space than it is getting? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-what-has-been-taking-up-space/)
- Think of a recent moment when you felt engaged, useful, connected, or quietly proud. What was happening? What mattered in that moment? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-what-was-happening/)
- What are you unwilling to let disappear beneath busyness, urgency, or other people’s expectations? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-what-you-refuse-to-let-disappear/)
- What are you trying to make room for? What would you need to protect, reduce, decline, or stop doing to create that room? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-what-are-you-making-room-for/)
- Which distractions, obligations, habits, or unresolved decisions repeatedly pull you away from what matters? Which are changeable, and which need to be accepted or worked around for now? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-the-pull-away-from-what-matters/)
- What responsibilities or relationships must remain cared for even if they are not your active focus this cycle? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-care-that-stays-off-center/)
- If this reflection helped you make one honest choice, what choice would you want it to clarify? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-what-choice-would-you-want-it-to-clarify/)

## 2. Choose your focus domains

Choose up to three active-focus domains for this cycle. The examples—health/capacity, work/contribution, finances, relationships/community, family, meaning/values/growth, recreation/creativity, and home/daily life—are invitations, not a fixed list. Rename, combine, replace, or ignore them. Other domains can be maintained or deferred without being unimportant.

For each area you might focus on:

- What part of life is asking for attention now? What makes this the right season to focus there? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-the-area-calling-for-attention/)
- What is happening in this area today? Describe a recent scene or concrete fact rather than giving it a label. [guide](../../../letters/crucible-the-present-state/)
- If you could choose freely, what would you call this domain? Does it overlap with another area, or should it be kept distinct? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-naming-your-domain/)
- What might happen if you focus here? What might it cost in time, energy, money, attention, or another commitment? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-the-possibilities-of-focus/)
- What would need to be maintained or protected elsewhere while this area receives focus? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-protecting-the-rest-of-life/)
- If you defer this area, what would make that a deliberate choice rather than neglect? What would tell you it is time to revisit it? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-deliberate-deferral/)

Now select no more than three active focuses. For each, answer the next four sections. Leave any section open when you do not yet know.

## 3. Give each focus a longer-term direction

### Longer-term vision

- Imagine this part of life moving in a direction that feels meaningful—not perfect. What is different in an ordinary day? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-ordinary-day-meaningful-direction/)
- What do you want to be more true here? What do you want to be less governed by? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-more-true-here/)
- Who else might experience the difference? What would they see, hear, or be able to count on? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-who-experiences-the-difference/)
- What value, need, or hope makes this direction worth moving toward? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-value-behind-the-direction/)
- Which parts are within your influence? Which outcomes depend on other people or conditions you cannot control? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-within-your-influence/)
- Write a few sentences beginning: “Over time, I want this part of my life to…” Keep it a compass, not a deadline or guarantee. [guide](../../../letters/crucible-over-time-i-want-this-part/)

### Role you want to embody

- In this domain, what role do you want to practice or grow into (for example, parent, friend, learner, steward, maker, colleague, partner, or advocate)? Use your own name for it. [guide](../../../letters/crucible-role-to-practice/)
- What does this role ask of you in a real situation? What do you want people to be able to count on you for? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-what-role-asks/)
- What would you do, say, make, or refrain from doing when you were living this role well? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-living-role-well/)
- Which role is already present but under-supported? Which role might you be ready to promote—give more deliberate attention, responsibility, or room? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-under-supported-role/)
- What role are you currently over-carrying, performing for approval, or trying to hold alone? What might a healthier version look like? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-over-carried-role/)

### Visible proof or first action

- What could you observe in the world that would suggest movement toward this vision? Name a behavior, conversation, artifact, decision, or change in conditions. [guide](../../../letters/crucible-what-could-you-observe-in-the-world/)
- What is one small action that could create useful evidence, even if it does not produce the whole outcome? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-one-small-action-useful-evidence/)
- What would count as partial progress? What evidence would challenge your assumption or teach you the direction needs revising? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-what-counts-as-partial-progress/)
- Where could you notice this evidence—in your calendar, a conversation, completed work, a felt change, or someone’s response? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-where-could-you-notice-this-evidence/)

### Edges to bring, develop, or balance

Use the nine Edges as a vocabulary for reflection, not as a checklist to complete or a scale to rate. For this domain, which qualities might help you inhabit the chosen role? Which need support or practice? Could a familiar strength be overused? Leave any Edge aside if it does not illuminate the situation.

- **Direction:** What deserves your attention here? What would help you choose a heading when several demands compete? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-direction-choose-a-heading/)
- **Craft:** What skill, quality of work, or deliberate practice would make your contribution more trustworthy? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-craft-trustworthy-contribution/)
- **Alignment:** Where do your actions match—or conflict with—your stated values and commitments? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-alignment-actions-values-commitments/)
- **Stewardship:** What resource, responsibility, relationship, or opportunity needs careful tending over time? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-stewardship-careful-tending/)
- **Adaptation:** What needs to change in response to the conditions, rather than being forced through the old approach? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-adaptation-change-with-conditions/)
- **Service:** Who or what is served by the role you want to play? How can you contribute without erasing your own limits? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-service-contribute-without-erasing-limits/)
- **Legacy:** What do you hope your choices build, pass on, or make possible beyond this immediate moment? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-legacy-beyond-the-immediate-moment/)
- **Integration:** What parts of your life or identity need to be brought into better relationship here? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-integration-parts-of-life-identity/)
- **Expression:** What truth, perspective, creativity, or preference needs a clearer voice? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-expression-truth-needs-a-clearer-voice/)

Then choose only the useful reflection:

- Which Edge seems needed for the next step? What specific behavior would express it? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-edge-needed-next-step/)
- Which quality is already available and worth relying on? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-quality-already-available/)
- Which quality feels difficult to access, and what practice, support, or condition might help? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-difficult-quality-access/)
- Is a strength being used so intensely that it creates a cost? What would balance look like? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-strength-used-intensely-cost/)
- What quality are you developing, and what small evidence would show practice rather than mastery? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-quality-developing/)
- Across your focus domains, what pattern belongs under “needed,” “balanced,” “overused,” or “developing”? [guide](../../../letters/crucible-pattern-across-focus-domains/)

## 4. Look across domains before you commit

- Where do the visions reinforce each other? What action in one domain could also support another?
- Where do they compete for time, attention, money, or emotional capacity? What is the actual trade-off—not the idealized version?
- What must not be neglected while you focus? What is the smallest reliable maintenance action or safeguard?
- Is there a tension you need to accept for now rather than solve? What would help you carry it consciously?
- What action could honor more than one vision or manage a trade-off without pretending it disappears?
- What is one meaningful next action that can begin this cycle?
- When or after what kind of cycle will you revisit the vision? What change in season or circumstance would justify returning sooner?

## 5. Turn vision into a weekly plan

Use the weekly questions after choosing your direction. Plan no more than three strikes; unused spaces are fine. A strike is a concrete action in service to something that matters, sized for the week you actually have.

For each possible strike, ask:

- Which focus domain does it serve? Which role and longer-term direction does it connect to?
- What is the one observable action—verb plus outcome—that I intend to take?
- Which one relevant Edge do I want to bring to this action? What would that quality look like in behavior?
- Is this **Establish** (creating new ground) or **Maintain** (sustaining progress already in motion)? Why is that effort appropriate?
- What realistic time, duration, appointment, or repeatable event will contain the action? What could disrupt that container?
- What evidence would tell me I attempted it, including a meaningful partial attempt?

Then size the plan as a whole:

- What time, energy, attention, or other capacity can I realistically give these strikes after existing responsibilities?
- What needs to be reduced or made smaller so the plan fits? Which tempting action should remain off the plan?
- What restoration or recovery will I protect because it helps make this work sustainable?
- Is one bounded relationship or community action important this week? What is a suitably limited action—or why is it not relevant now?
- What is this plan in service to? Who or what benefits if I follow through?
- What one boundary, refusal, or defer decision will protect the plan? What specifically will I decline, not add, or let wait?
- If conditions change, what is the smallest responsible revision rather than an all-or-nothing response?

## 6. Notice evidence during the week (optional)

The Weekly Domain Blotter is optional. You can use a calendar, notes, memory, or another simple record instead. Evidence is for understanding the plan in real conditions—not for accumulating points, streaks, or proof of personal worth.

- What did I plan, in the words I used? Which domain and role did each strike serve?
- For each day that matters, did the action happen, happen partly, or not happen? Is a blank more accurate because I did not track it or did not expect the action that day?
- What helped the strike happen? What changed, interrupted, or made it easier than expected?
- What do the marks or notes show when I add the context behind them?
- Which one moment would be useful to revisit—not to blame myself, but to understand the conditions or choice?
- What did the record miss? What do I know from memory, conversation, or the surrounding circumstances?

Bring evidence into the Weekly Role Review. Bring one useful friction moment, if there is one, into Weekly Recalibration. If the blotter adds burden rather than insight, leave it unused.

## 7. Review how you showed up in your roles

At week’s end, choose up to three domain/role combinations worth reviewing. Use concrete evidence; you do not need to explain every event through an Edge.

For each role:

- Which domain and role am I reviewing? What did I intend to do in that role?
- What did I actually do or say? Recall a specific event, action, or conversation.
- Did the planned action happen: **Yes**, **Partly**, **Not yet**, or **N/A**? What context makes that answer accurate?
- Which Edge or quality helped? How did it show up in what I did?
- What felt lacking or needed—an Edge, skill, boundary, resource, or support?
- Did a strength become overused? What did that cost me or someone else?

Then step back:

- What behavior, Edge, or condition appeared more than once? Is there enough evidence to call it a pattern, or is it one event to keep observing?
- What do I want to keep because it served the role? What needs balancing, support, or repair?
- Is there a repair or conversation that belongs before I move on? What would a respectful, specific repair look like?
- Which one role would benefit from practice next week? What observable action would express the Edge I want to practice there?
- Does this seem like an individual practice question, or should I examine structural friction—time, load, clarity, support, recovery, or boundary—before setting the next plan?

## 8. Recalibrate one friction point

Choose one event that offers useful information. Keep the vision as direction; adjust one workable part of the next week rather than redesigning your life.

### Separate what happened from what it means

- What happened, in observable facts? When and where? Which role and domain were involved?
- What had I intended to do? What did I actually do, including any partial action, alternative, or decision not to proceed?
- What changed between intention and action? What part of the sequence is clear, and what remains uncertain?
- What feeling or body response did I notice at the time?
- What protective story, fear, assumption, or unmet need appeared? What might it be trying to protect? What evidence supports or challenges that story?
- Which Edge helped, if any? What felt difficult to access, or overused? What support might have changed the experience?

### Notice impact and structure

- What was the effect on time or capacity? On progress or quality? On wellbeing or connection? Was there another important effect?
- Which conditions contributed: unclear strike or next action; no scheduled container; overcommitment; capacity or recovery; unclear boundary; missing support; an Edge misapplied or unbalanced; something else?
- Which part was within my agency? Which part depended on constraints or other people? What should not be placed on one person’s shoulders?
- What setup, load, timing, support, recovery, or boundary could change the odds next time?

### Choose one bounded adjustment

- What will I change in the domain action or role—and what will I leave unchanged because it still fits?
- Which Edge cue or practice could support the adjusted action? What brief reminder would help in the moment?
- What realistic time container and trigger will I use?
- What boundary or defer choice protects this adjustment? What support or recovery does it need?
- When will I try it? What evidence or track point will tell me how it went?
- When will I review that evidence? What would count as “helped,” “partly helped,” or “needs another adjustment” without turning the result into a verdict?

Carry the adjustment into a fresh Weekly Domain Plan. Test the conditions and action; do not reset your worth or discard a direction that still matters because one week was difficult.

## 9. Step back every fourth weekly cycle

Every fourth weekly cycle, complete the Monthly Domain Review in addition to the regular weekly plan, evidence, role review, and recalibration. Use the month and week/date range that match the evidence you actually have.

### Compare direction and evidence

- Which domains were in focus? What did I hope would move in them?
- What time and actions in my calendar aligned with that direction? What meaningful action might a calendar fail to show?
- What gap, progress, or changed condition matters most? What is evidence, and what is my interpretation?
- What capacity, rest, or recovery helped? What relief, recovery, or support is needed now?
- What practical adjustment to workload, support, recovery, or rhythm would make the next month more workable?

### Review roles, Edges, agency, and friction

- Across the month, what specific evidence shows how I carried my responsibilities or participated in important roles?
- Which Edges repeatedly helped? Which felt difficult to access? Which strength or behavior became costly when overused?
- Is this pattern repeated across enough situations to be useful, or should I keep observing?
- Where did I have agency—choices, actions, boundaries, or requests I could make, including small ones?
- What pressure or friction recurred? What structural cause, resource, or support could ease it?
- What needs to be kept, balanced, repaired, or made possible before I add another priority?

### Choose next month’s priorities

Choose up to three domain/role priorities. Mark each **Carry**, **Change**, or **Defer**. Leave unused spaces open.

- What is the domain and role? How does this priority connect to the longer-term direction?
- Should I carry it because it still matters, change it because evidence or circumstances call for a different form, or defer it without declaring it unimportant?
- What outcome or milestone would be meaningful this month without pretending I control every result?
- What support rhythm, recovery practice, or boundary will protect the work?
- What first visible action can someone observe?
- Which repeated pattern or Edge observation matters to these priorities?
- When is the monthly review point? Which next weekly plan will translate the priorities into strikes, and what is the first step?

## 10. Assemble your working vision document

Use your answers to make a short reference you can bring into later Crucible work. It is a living draft, not a contract. Keep details vivid enough that you can recognize the direction in ordinary choices.

### My season and direction

- **This season of life:** What is true about my current circumstances and capacity?
- **What matters most now:** What do I want this work to serve?
- **Longer-term direction:** “Over time, I want…”
- **Space I will protect:** What must I reduce, decline, defer, or make room for?
- **What must not be neglected:** Which responsibilities or areas need a maintenance safeguard?

### My focus domains and roles

For each of up to three domains, record:

- **Domain:** What I choose to call this area.
- **Vision:** What I want to become more true over time.
- **Role to promote:** How I want to participate and what people may count on me for.
- **Visible evidence:** What I could observe that would show movement.
- **First action:** One concrete next move.
- **Edge(s) to hone:** The quality or practice I want to develop, with a behavior that expresses it.
- **Edge(s) to rely on or balance:** What helps, what needs support, and what strength I want to moderate.
- **Maintenance or defer choice:** What I will keep cared for, or deliberately revisit later.

### Connections and constraints

- **Visions that reinforce each other:**
- **Tension or trade-off I accept and will manage:**
- **Integrating action:**
- **Capacity and restoration conditions:**
- **Boundary, refusal, or defer choice:**
- **Support or relationship/community action, if relevant:**

### How I will put it to work

- **Next weekly strikes:** Up to three domain/role-linked actions, each with a relevant Edge, Establish/Maintain choice, and time container.
- **Evidence I will notice:** What I will observe, without scoring myself.
- **Role practice:** One observable action that expresses a quality I want to bring.
- **If friction appears:** The structural conditions I will check and one bounded adjustment I may test.
- **Review point:** When I will revisit the weekly evidence and when I will step back for monthly review.

Read this document and ask: “Does this sound like a direction I choose, in a life I actually have?” Revise it when your season changes or evidence gives you a reason—not as punishment for an imperfect week. Carry the relevant parts into the worksheets: select which Edge to practice, which role deserves attention, and which strike fits the coming week.
