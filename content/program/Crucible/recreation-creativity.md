## Applying the nine Edges across the candidate life domains

Use these as applications of the book’s Edge concepts to candidate domains, not as prescriptions from the book. Red — brittle/dull describes an Edge that is weak or unused, or has become rigid, excessive, or distorted. Yellow — mixed/in progress describes a nonjudgmental, context-dependent or developing expression, not a score or diagnosis. Green — strong/sharp describes an Edge that is formed, useful, and responsive—not turned up to maximum. For each domain, compare what you experience internally with what you do or show externally.

### Recreation & creativity

| Edge | Red — brittle/dull — internal; external | Yellow — mixed/in progress — internal; external | Green — strong/sharp — internal; external |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direction | You feel aimless or demand every hobby be productive; you never begin or turn recreation into a metric. | You are still clarifying what matters in this area; some choices follow a priority, while others are shaped by immediate demands. (for example, depending on the season or circumstances). | You know what kinds of play restore, delight, or challenge you; you make room instead of waiting for leftover time. |
| Craft | You fear being bad or insist on mastery before enjoyment; you abandon hobbies early or train until play disappears. | You are building confidence through practice; some routines feel workable, while others still need learning or support. (for example, depending on the season or circumstances). | You enjoy being a beginner and practicing for its own sake; you build skill through playful repetition. |
| Alignment | You feel guilty for pleasure or justify every indulgence; you deny restorative play or pursue activities against your values and limits. | You are weighing your values against real constraints; some choices feel congruent, while others remain unsettled. (for example, depending on the season or circumstances). | Your recreation reflects genuine enjoyment and values; you choose activities that fit your desired life. |
| Stewardship | You treat play as frivolous or fear any constraint; you neglect recreation or let it crowd out responsibilities and rest. | You are tending this area unevenly; a few resources or responsibilities are managed intentionally, while others need attention. (for example, depending on the season or circumstances). | You see play and creative energy as needing care; you protect time and tools without letting hobbies consume every resource. |
| Adaptation | You cling to an old identity or lose direction at every dip; you force a hobby that no longer fits or constantly restart. | You are adjusting to changing circumstances; some responses fit the new situation, while others still reflect the old one. (for example, depending on the season or circumstances). | You let interests evolve without calling it failure; you change medium, pace, or activity as conditions and curiosity shift. |
| Service | You feel recreation must justify itself by helping others or that sharing ruins ownership; you monetize every hobby or refuse shared enjoyment. | You are finding a workable balance between contributing and respecting capacity; what feels appropriate may vary by situation. (for example, depending on the season or circumstances). | You let enjoyment nourish generosity without turning it into obligation; you share skills when welcome while keeping play alive for yourself. |
| Legacy | You dismiss what you make or obsess over being remembered; you hide all work or create only for recognition. | You are noticing longer-term effects, though immediate needs still shape some choices and patterns are continuing to develop. (for example, depending on the season or circumstances). | You see that play and creative work can leave traces; you create memories, artifacts, or traditions that may enrich others. |
| Integration | You feel pleasure must be earned or use it to escape life; you never make room for play or let it become avoidance. | You are connecting this area to some parts of your life more clearly than others; you are working out a sustainable fit. | You see play as part of a full life, not a reward after everything; recreation renews capacity and coexists with other domains. |
| Expression | You hide unfinished work or depend on praise; you suppress creativity or shape everything for approval. | You are sharing some needs or ideas more readily than others; comfort and clarity may depend on the situation. (for example, depending on the season or circumstances). | You create and let work be seen without requiring universal approval; you share, perform, make, or play authentically. |
