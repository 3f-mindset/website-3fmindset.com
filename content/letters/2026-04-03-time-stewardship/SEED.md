Week 14: Learn from the Masters (Seek Mentorship & Knowledge)
Lecture: Even the greatest swordsmen learned from a master or studied techniques from those who came before. This week focuses on mentorship and continuous learning. A man grounded in mastery recognizes he doesn’t have all the answers and actively seeks wisdom from others more experienced. Discuss the value of finding mentors – people who have achieved what you aspire to – and learning from them. That can mean an in-person mentor, a coach, or even “virtual” mentors through books, lectures, and biographies. Encourage participants to drop any ego that prevents them from asking for guidance or investing time in learning. Direct tone: If you want to cut years off your learning curve, learn from those who’ve already walked the path. Also, emphasize building a “library” of knowledge – reading good books or taking courses is essentially mentorship at scale. This week, each man will identify at least one mentor or knowledge source to tap into. By learning from masters, you ground yourself in proven principles and accelerate your journey to mastery.
Action: Reach out to a mentor or begin studying a master in a field you want to improve in. This week, take a concrete step to learn from someone more experienced.
Steps to Take:
1. Identify a Mentor or Source: Think of an area where you want to grow (fitness, career, relationships, etc.). Identify one person who exemplifies excellence in that area. If it’s someone you know personally and respect, great – they could be a potential mentor. If not, find an author, expert, or public figure renowned in that field whose knowledge you can access (through books, interviews, online courses).
2. Make Contact or Commit to Study: If it’s a real-life person you can talk to, muster the courage to reach out this week – send an email or ask them for a coffee meeting, expressing your desire to learn from their experience. Have a few specific questions in mind to show you value their time. If it’s a book or course, obtain it (buy the book, sign up for the webinar, etc.) and block time in your schedule to start engaging with that material daily.
3. Extract and Apply Wisdom: As you connect with or study this “master,” take notes on key lessons or principles that stand out. Most importantly, choose at least one piece of advice or technique you will implement immediately. For example, if your chosen mentor (or book) emphasizes a certain training regimen, try it out; if they describe a mindset that made them successful, practice thinking that way in your context. Knowledge only becomes power when applied. Plan a follow-up with real mentors to update them on your progress (this keeps you accountable and shows them you’re serious).
Reflection: “What is one invaluable lesson I learned from my mentor or study this week, and how can I integrate that lesson into my daily life moving forward?”
