You are not lazy. You are not broken. You are just alone.

This week, we are tackling the silent killer of most good intentions: the vacuum of willpower. We often think discipline is a muscle that we can squeeze until it holds. But in reality, discipline is a fire, and fire needs air. When you rely only on your own memory and mood, you are trying to burn a fire in a vacuum. You have the fuel and the hearth, but without the air of external pressure, the coal just sits there, cold and dark.

The attached lesson, "The Simple Check-In That Keeps You From Quitting," explores why we resist being seen. We are afraid of the mirror. We are afraid that if someone sees us, they will see that we are not as disciplined as we think we are. But the embarrassment of a missed check-in is a small sting. The cost of staying alone is large. When you miss a goal in silence, you feel nothing. You just adjust. You say, "Well, I didn't do it," and you move on. The fire dies quietly. No one notices. No one cares. You are the only one who knows.

You need one clear person, one clear time, and one clear report. This is the check-in. It is a small moment where you say, "Here is what I did." It turns a vague goal into a hard fact. It makes your next step hard to skip because someone is waiting for it.
