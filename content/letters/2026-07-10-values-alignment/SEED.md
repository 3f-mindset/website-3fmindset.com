Week 28: Values Alignment Check – Are You Living Your Principles?
Lecture: We identified core values back in Week 2; now it’s time to inspect how well your life aligns with them. A strong forge (life) is one where actions and values are in harmony – that’s when you burn hottest with purpose. If there’s misalignment (saying family is a top value but never being present, valuing honesty but finding yourself lying at work), the forge is out of tune, and it weakens the blade (you) with internal conflict or guilt. Speak earnestly and challenge the group: “Do your daily choices reflect what you claim to stand for?” This week involves reflection and possibly tough realization. The goal is not to shame but to course-correct. Reiterate the importance of values as a compass – if we’re off-course, we adjust, not throw away the compass. Encourage concrete examples: if health is a stated value but one’s lifestyle is unhealthy, acknowledge it and plan change. If growth is a value but one spends more time in comfort activities than learning, time to pivot. This week’s task: pick one value you hold and identify one discrepancy in how you live it, then take an action to realign (or, if you discover a value you thought you had isn’t truly yours, adjust your understanding).
Action: Choose one of your core values and honestly assess how well your current behavior fulfills it. If there’s a gap, commit to a specific action that will bring you closer to living that value consistently, starting this week.
Steps to Take:
1. Select a Value to Inspect: From the core values you defined (Week 2), pick one that you suspect you might not be fully honoring lately. Maybe it’s “health,” “family,” “integrity,” “excellence,” etc.
2. List Current Behaviors: Write down a few ways you have been supporting this value and a few ways you’ve been falling short. Be brutally honest. For example, Value: Health. Supporting behaviors: go to gym 3x a week. Undermining behaviors: eat fast food lunch daily, sleep only 5 hours. Or Value: Family. Supporting: home for dinner most nights. Undermining: rarely engage in meaningful conversation because always checking work emails, missed daughter’s game due to optional work function. This contrast will show where alignment is strong and where it’s weak.
3. Realign with Action: Identify one meaningful change to better live that value. It could be stopping a negative (e.g., “No work emails after 7pm so I’m present with family,” or “Cut weekday fast food, prep healthy lunches instead”) or adding a positive (e.g., “Spend Saturday morning doing an activity with my kids,” or “Schedule a yearly doctor check-up I’ve been avoiding”). Implement this change immediately and treat it as non-negotiable, since it’s tied to your core principle. Notice how living in alignment feels – usually it brings a sense of integrity and strength. The aim is to reduce internal conflicts between what we say matters and what we actually do. Over time, repeat this exercise for other values until your life more fully reflects what you stand for. A man whose forge is aligned with his values burns with a steady, powerful flame.
Reflection: “Which core value did I find a gap in living out? What concrete step did I take to live that value more fully, and what difference did that make in my sense of self or satisfaction this week? Where else in my life can I tighten the alignment between my beliefs and my actions?”
