Week 25: Relationships Audit – Friends and Influences
Lecture: The people around you are the coals and hammers of your forge, shaping you daily. This week, we scrutinize relationships and social influences. It’s often said you are the average of the five people you spend the most time with. In a direct but respectful tone, address the hard truth: some relationships propel us forward, while others hold us back or even poison our progress. Encourage participants to examine their circle – friends, coworkers, even family – and assess who consistently supports, energizes, or challenges them to be better, versus who drains, discourages, or tempts them into bad habits. Discuss setting boundaries with negative influences and seeking out more positive ones (like mentors, supportive peers, or groups aligned with their goals). Also include the influence of media they consume: social media feeds, news, entertainment – these can act like virtual “friends” influencing mood and mindset. The idea is not to judge people harshly, but to be intentional about one’s social environment. This week’s task is to take one concrete step in upgrading the quality of influences: that might mean having a tough conversation, reducing contact with a toxic person, or reaching out to someone inspiring to spend more time with them.
Action: Evaluate your five closest relationships (or most frequent influences) and identify one change to make: either limit a toxic influence or deepen a positive one. Implement that change this week.
Steps to Take:
1. List Key People: Write down the five people you interact with the most (in person or significant communication). These could be friends, colleagues, family members, or even online personalities you follow heavily. Beside each name, note the general impact on you: Do you feel motivated, supported, and positive after being around them? Or drained, irritated, or pulled into unhelpful behaviors? Also consider: do they encourage your growth or roll their eyes at your aspirations?
2. Determine an Adjustment: Choose one relationship that needs change. If someone is a net negative influence – constantly negative, disrespectful, or enabling your bad habits – decide on a boundary or reduction plan (e.g., not discussing certain topics with them, spending less time, or in extreme cases, cutting ties if possible). Conversely, identify a positive person you want to learn from or be around more, and plan how to cultivate that (e.g., invite that disciplined coworker to work out together, schedule a call with that encouraging old friend more regularly).
3. Take Action in Relationship: Put the change into practice. For reducing a negative: it could be as simple as not answering toxic rants in group chats, politely declining a hangout that usually ends in trouble, or being honest that you’re focusing on personal growth and won’t be engaging in XYZ anymore. You don’t have to create drama; just quietly create distance or limits. For increasing a positive: reach out with a message or call, expressing you value them and setting up time to talk or meet. People who uplift you will likely be happy to connect more. As you do this, you might feel some discomfort (especially with pulling back from a friend), but recall that you’re protecting the man you are becoming. Over time, you’ll see the benefit: more energy and positivity, less sabotage.
Reflection: “Which relationship in my life most needs a change and why? What steps did I take to address this, and how do I anticipate it will improve my growth and peace of mind moving forward?”
