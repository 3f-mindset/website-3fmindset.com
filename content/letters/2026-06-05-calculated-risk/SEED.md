Week 23: Your Origin Story – Reflect on Your Past
Lecture: Every man’s forge was lit long before he realized – in his childhood and formative years. This week, we delve into your origin story. By understanding where you come from and what experiences have shaped you, you gain insight into why you think and act as you do today. Speak in a reflective yet firm tone: it’s time to shine a light into the past, not to dwell or make excuses, but to extract understanding and perhaps to lay some ghosts to rest. Prompt the group to consider key events, family dynamics, and cultural influences that have left a mark on their identity. For example, how did your parents’ values or behavior influence your own? What pivotal moments – a triumph, a trauma, a move, a mentor, a failure – stand out as turning points? Emphasize taking the stance of an observer or storyteller of your own life. This is your hero’s backstory; knowing it helps you see the narrative you’ve been carrying. Encourage honesty – sometimes men avoid confronting the past, but clarity here is power. This week’s action is to write down a brief narrative of your life’s key shaping moments and identify how they influence you today.
Action: Write a brief “origin story” of yourself, highlighting 3–5 key life events or influences from your past that have significantly forged who you are. Identify one way each has impacted your beliefs or behavior today (for better or worse).
Steps to Take:
1. Recall Key Chapters: Divide your life into a few rough phases (childhood, adolescence, early adulthood, recent years). In each phase, pinpoint major events or influences: family situation, school experiences, successes, failures, relocations, relationships, losses, etc. List the ones that feel most impactful.
2. Connect Cause and Effect: For each key event or influence, jot down how you think it affected you. For example: “Grew up with a strict father – I became disciplined but also struggle with showing emotion,” or “Parents’ divorce when I was 10 – led me to fear abandonment in relationships,” or “Coach Smith believed in me in high school – I learned confidence and the value of hard work.” Be frank about both the strengths and scars your past has given you.
3. Write Your Story: Compose 2–3 paragraphs (or bullet points if easier) that tell the story of you, focusing on those key elements. Imagine you’re giving a new friend the summary of what made you who you are. After writing, step back and read it. Recognize that while you didn’t choose those initial conditions, you are now the author going forward. This awareness is crucial: you see which parts of your forge were hot or cold, supportive or damaging. You can appreciate the positive shaping forces and acknowledge the negative ones that you might need to counteract.
Reflection: “Looking back at my origin story, what is one pattern or belief I picked up from my past that still affects me? Is this serving me or do I need to let it go or change it as I move forward?”
