## Post

The mug is still on the workbench. The notebook is open. One line is written: Save $5,000 by December 31. The pen is in your hand, but the next task is not on the page. You close the notebook and tell yourself Monday will be different. Monday arrives. The goal is still there. The next task is still missing.

That is the stuck feeling behind the goal. Not a lack of fire. Not a lack of ambition. A goal with no shape can live in your head for months and still not move. It waits for the right mood, the free weekend, the moment you feel ready. Then life takes the first slot in the week, and the goal slips again.

The move is to give the goal a shape. A number. A deadline. Dated milestones. Weekly actions. A simple review that keeps the plan alive when motivation fades. The worksheet is the execution tool for the week's transformation. It is the page you will actually work from: one goal, a deadline, three milestones, and the first task you can do this week.

The lesson and lecture will support the worksheet, not replace it. They will help you see how to use the page well, how to keep the plan from becoming another list you abandon, and how to adjust when life gets busy. The worksheet is the practical artifact the group will be working from.

By the time we meet, I want you to have more than a goal. I want you to have a plan you can show. A number. A date. A first task. When we talk, we will not just discuss what you want. We will look at what you have built, where it is weak, and what the next move is.

Do the worksheet before the discussion. Bring the page, not just the intention. The mug is still on the bench. The notebook is no longer blank. The next task is waiting for you to put it down on paper.
