# REFINE PROCESS GUIDE

## Introduction

Most men think their biggest problem is a lack of discipline. More often, the real problem is that they are standing in the wrong forge.

You become like whatever you spend time around. That includes your friends, your coworkers, your family, your social media feed, the podcasts you play during your commute, and even the group chats you open every day. Every influence is either feeding your fire or stealing heat from it.

The difficult part is that influence is almost invisible. You rarely notice yourself changing in a single conversation. Instead, hundreds of small moments slowly become your beliefs, your standards, your habits, and eventually your identity.

This worksheet is designed to make the invisible visible.

Do not rush through it. There are no perfect answers, only honest ones. You are not judging people. You are inspecting your forge. The goal is not to control everyone around you. The goal is to become intentional about who and what gets repeated access to your attention.

Complete each section in order. Resist the urge to jump ahead. Every step builds on the one before it.

---

# R: REVIEW

Everything begins with awareness.

Before you can improve your relationships, you need an honest picture of who is already shaping your life. Most people underestimate how much influence comes through repeated exposure rather than dramatic moments.

As you fill out this section, think about the people and media you spend the most time with, not necessarily the people you like the most. Frequency matters more than intensity.

Include family, friends, coworkers, coaches, podcasts, YouTube channels, social media accounts, news sources, or anyone whose voice regularly enters your thinking.

Do not worry about whether your answers make someone look good or bad. Your responsibility is accuracy.

**Strong answers look like:**

- "My brother encourages me to stay disciplined with my workouts."
- "My coworker complains about management every day."
- "I spend an hour every night watching creators who teach business."
- "I open social media every morning before work."

**Avoid answers like:**

- "Everyone is fine."
- "Nobody really affects me."
- "It's complicated."
- Vague opinions without examples.

Ask yourself:

- Who gets the most access to my attention?
- Who influences my decisions?
- Who shapes my standards without me noticing?

---

# E: EVALUATE

Now look beyond names.

Your goal is not to decide whether someone is a good or bad person. Your goal is to understand the effect they have on you.

Every influence produces a result.

Ask yourself what usually happens after spending time with each person or consuming each source of media.

Do you leave stronger?

Or weaker?

More focused?

Or distracted?

More hopeful?

Or more cynical?

Patterns matter far more than isolated moments.

**Strong evaluations include:**

- "I usually leave motivated to improve."
- "We always end up gossiping."
- "This podcast gives me practical ideas."
- "This group chat wastes an hour every evening."

**Avoid evaluations like:**

- Judging someone's character instead of their influence.
- Making excuses because you've known someone for years.
- Focusing only on how much you enjoy them.
- Ignoring your own responsibility.

Remember that enjoyable and helpful are not always the same thing.

---

# F: FILTER

This is where awareness becomes action.

Not every influence deserves unlimited access to your life.

Some relationships simply need healthier boundaries.

Some media should only be consumed occasionally.

Some conversations should end sooner.

Some influences deserve much more of your attention.

Filtering is not rejection.

It is prioritization.

You are deciding what helps your future and what quietly pulls you backward.

**Strong filters include:**

- Limiting negative conversations.
- Unfollowing accounts that constantly fuel comparison.
- Turning off unnecessary notifications.
- Spending less time in environments that encourage bad habits.

**Avoid filtering by:**

- Trying to change everyone else.
- Creating unnecessary conflict.
- Making emotional decisions out of anger.
- Believing boundaries require drama.

Healthy boundaries often happen quietly.

---

# I: INVEST

Removing unhealthy influences creates space.

Now you must intentionally fill that space with better ones.

Growth accelerates when you spend more time around people already living the life you want to build.

Choose one relationship worth strengthening.

Reach out.

Ask questions.

Schedule time together.

Listen more than you speak.

People who consistently improve themselves often enjoy helping someone who genuinely wants to learn.

**Strong investments include:**

- Scheduling coffee with a mentor.
- Asking a disciplined friend to work out together.
- Joining a group built around growth.
- Reading or listening to someone who teaches practical wisdom.

**Avoid investments like:**

- Waiting for others to contact you first.
- Assuming successful people are too busy.
- Looking only for inspiration instead of accountability.
- Staying passive.

Better relationships rarely happen by accident.

Someone has to take the first step.

---

# N: NAVIGATE

Not every difficult relationship can disappear.

Family, coworkers, clients, and neighbors will often remain part of your life.

The goal is to learn how to interact without allowing unhealthy influence to control your direction.

Boundaries protect your future.

You can stay respectful while becoming less available for conversations that damage your mindset.

You do not owe unlimited access to everyone.

**Healthy navigation might include:**

- Changing the subject when conversations become toxic.
- Politely declining invitations that repeatedly lead to poor decisions.
- Spending less time in draining environments.
- Limiting exposure without creating unnecessary conflict.

**Avoid:**

- Starting arguments to prove a point.
- Trying to fix everyone.
- Feeling guilty for protecting your growth.
- Believing distance equals disrespect.

Sometimes the strongest boundary is simply changing where your attention goes.

---

# E: EXPAND

Growth is never permanent.

Relationships change.

Influences change.

You change.

That is why this audit should become a regular practice instead of a one-time exercise.

Every month, ask yourself whether your circle still reflects the man you are becoming.

If not, adjust.

Continue investing in healthy relationships.

Continue protecting your attention.

Continue removing influences that slowly pull you backward.

Small improvements repeated over time completely reshape your environment.

**Strong expansion habits include:**

- Repeating this audit every month.
- Intentionally meeting new growth-minded people.
- Reviewing your media habits regularly.
- Looking for mentors in every season of life.

**Avoid:**

- Assuming your environment will stay healthy forever.
- Returning to old influences simply because they are familiar.
- Believing one good week solves the problem permanently.

Your relationships will either reinforce your identity or reshape it.

Choose which direction they are pulling you.

---

## Final Thought

Every forge produces something.

The only question is whether it produces strength or weakness.

When you finish this worksheet, you should not simply know more about your relationships.

You should know exactly which influences deserve greater access to your life, which ones require boundaries, and what your next action will be.

A stronger future rarely begins with finding better circumstances.

It begins with choosing better influences.
