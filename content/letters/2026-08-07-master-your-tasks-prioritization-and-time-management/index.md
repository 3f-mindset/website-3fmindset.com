## Your List Is Not Your Leader

You do not have a task problem first. You have a decision problem.

When every request enters your day with the same weight, the loudest thing wins. The notification, the easy errand, and someone else’s poor planning take the place of the work that would actually move your life forward.

Busy is not a result. A full list is not proof that you used your day well.

This week, sort the work before the work sorts you. Capture every open loop, choose the three things that would make the day count, and give them a protected place on your calendar. Then make an honest decision about everything else: delete it, defer it, delegate it, or batch it.

## The Three Big Rocks

Your day does not need twenty priorities. It needs three clear ones.

Write each rock as an outcome, not a mood. “Send the proposal” is a task. “Work on the proposal” is fog. Put the first rock in your strongest available block. Turn off the noise. Let the easy tasks wait while the important thing receives your best attention.

That is the SORT system:

- **Survey** the full load.
- **Order** it by consequence.
- **Reserve** focused time.
- **Terminate** what does not deserve prime attention.

Run it for seven days. At the end of each day, ask what moved, what stole your attention, and what you will decide differently tomorrow.

Your task list is not your leader. You are.
