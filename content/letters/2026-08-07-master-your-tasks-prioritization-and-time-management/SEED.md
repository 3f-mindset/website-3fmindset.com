## Core Tension

He is busy because everything feels urgent, but the work that matters keeps getting pushed aside. His task list is a storage bin, not a decision system.

## Reader

Men who have goals, responsibilities, and too many open loops. He tends to react to the loudest request, confuse motion with progress, and protect low-value tasks because they are easy to finish.

## Promise

Use a simple daily sorting system to choose three big rocks, reserve focused time for them, and deliberately cut or defer the rest for seven days.

## Useful Scenes Or Stories

Inbox notifications, a phone beside the laptop, a full calendar, the task that keeps moving to tomorrow, the relief of crossing off a meaningful task before lunch, Eisenhower urgent/important quadrants, big rocks, batching, delegation, and Pomodoro focus blocks.

## Language To Use

“Your list is not your leader.” “If everything is priority, nothing is.” “Do the important thing before the easy thing.” “A man who cannot choose his work will be chosen by everyone else’s work.” “Busy is not a result.”

## Language To Avoid

Avoid productivity-bro hype, waking at 5 AM, pretending every task can fit, shame about unfinished work, complicated app worship, and generic advice to “work smarter.”

## Constraints

Date the public letter 2026-08-07. Use direct, grounded SteadyBurn voice. Build the worksheet around the new actionable verb SORT. Keep the offer practical: no total-life overhaul, only a seven-day priority experiment.

## Raw Notes

Master-guide seed: Week 32: Master Your Tasks – Prioritization and Time Management. Teach that structure means doing the right things in the right order. Introduce the Eisenhower Matrix, big rocks, batching, capture systems, saying no, delegation, and focused work sprints. The weekly action is to identify the top three tasks each day, tackle them first or reserve prime time, and evaluate daily.
