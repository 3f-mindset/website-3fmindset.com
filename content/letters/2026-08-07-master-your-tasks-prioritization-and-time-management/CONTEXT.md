## Actionable VERB
CHART

## Model
C - Capture: Write down every task, big or small, in one notebook or app. Do not trust your memory. A full list stops mental clutter and gives you a clear view of what actually needs doing.
H - Hierarchy: Sort the list by impact. Pick the three tasks that move your goals forward. Mark the rest as second-tier or minor. This step stops urgent noise from stealing your best hours.
A - Assign: Place the top three tasks into early morning time blocks. Treat these blocks like hard meetings. Put smaller tasks into later windows when your energy drops. This creates a predictable daily flow.
R - Restrict: Protect your focus blocks. Silence your phone, close extra browser tabs, and tell your team you are in deep work. Learn to defer or drop tasks that do not match your top three. This keeps your plan intact.
T - Track: Review your day before you stop working. Check if the top three are done. Move unfinished items forward only if they still matter. Cut the rest. This builds a reliable system that improves every week.

## Action Steps Summary
1. Dump and Sort: Clear your mind by listing every task, then rank them by real impact instead of loud urgency.
2. Block and Shield: Place your top three priorities into early morning time slots and lock down your environment to prevent distractions.
3. Review and Reset: Check your daily results, carry forward only what truly matters, and cut the rest to build a repeatable weekly rhythm.

## FAQ
Why do I feel busy but never finish what matters? You are likely chasing loud tasks instead of important ones. Pick three high-impact items first and protect their time.
How do I say no without sounding rude? Treat your schedule like a shared calendar. Reply with a clear time frame or offer a smaller alternative. Boundaries build respect.
Why does my to-do list grow faster than I can work? You are capturing tasks but not filtering them. Review your list daily, delete what does not move your goals, and batch small items.
How can I stop working late every night? You are probably doing low-value work during high-energy hours. Move your hardest tasks to the morning and group minor tasks for the afternoon.
What if my plan falls apart mid-day? Plans break when they lack a backup order. Keep a short list of fallback tasks and shift them to low-energy windows. Flexibility beats perfection.
