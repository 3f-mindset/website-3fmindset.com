## Subject

Finish the Work That Matters First

## Body

You can work all day and still leave the work that matters most unfinished. This week, you will build a practical system for choosing your priorities before urgency, noise, and other people’s requests choose them for you.

Your attached lesson, **“Finish the Work That Matters First,”** explains why busy days often produce little meaningful progress. When every task receives the same rank, small requests and easy wins crowd out the work that supports your goals, protects your responsibilities, or changes the shape of your future. The answer is not to do everything faster. It is to create an order of attack.

The lesson shows you how to capture your full workload in one trusted place, choose the three tasks that would create the most useful result, define what “done” means, and give those priorities protected space on your calendar. It also explains how to batch lesser tasks, guard your focus, delegate responsibly, and review unfinished work without turning it into a judgment about your character.

The worksheet is your central execution artifact for the week. Use it to run the TRIAGE process:

- **Track Every Task** in one trusted list.
- **Rank by Impact** and choose exactly three big rocks.
- **Isolate the Big Rocks** by defining a finish line and first physical action.
- **Assign Time** with realistic calendar blocks.
- **Guard Focus** with concrete boundaries against interruption.
- **Examine and Adjust** at the end of each day.

Complete the worksheet with your actual responsibilities, not an idealized version of your workload. Include the small tasks, unfinished projects, recurring duties, personal goals, household obligations, messages, errands, and promises that are competing for your attention. Then make the decisions your system requires: what will happen later, what will be delegated, and what should be removed.

The practical goal is to complete all three priority tasks on at least five of the next seven days. This is not a productivity exercise you finish once and forget. It is a weekly system-building practice. You are learning to turn pressure into completed work, give important responsibilities a place in your real life, and begin each day with a clear first move instead of reacting to whatever appears next.

The worksheet is designed to produce an executable plan: three ranked results, clear starting actions, protected time blocks, focus boundaries, and a review decision for anything unfinished. The lesson provides the reasoning and examples. The worksheet turns that understanding into action.

Use the GPT coach alongside the worksheet when you need help making a task concrete, choosing between competing priorities, defining a finish line, or creating a realistic time block. It will guide you through one TRIAGE step at a time and challenge vague answers so your plan is specific enough to use. You can ask it for support without handing over the decisions that belong to you.

This week’s public letter, **“Finish the Work That Matters First,”** gives you the wider context for the practice. Read it to understand why treating every request as equally important leaves you active but unfinished, and why a ranked task list can give your responsibilities a dependable path. The letter will help you see this week’s work as part of a larger transformation: moving from scattered reaction to deliberate execution.

Read the newsletter, complete the worksheet, and begin with the next seven days in front of you. You do not need perfect conditions or endless motivation. You need to choose what matters, give it time, protect that time, and review the evidence.

The loudest request does not have to run your day. Capture the full load, choose your three, and finish the work that matters first.
