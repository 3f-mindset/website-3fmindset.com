# Domain Vision + Edges Snapshot Worksheet

## Goal

Create a five-step planning sequence consistent with the existing season-design worksheets, with an optional daily blotter between weekly planning and review. The domain-vision snapshot helps you choose a handful of life domains, form a vision across selected domains, identify the roles that serve the vision, and use the nine Edges as a reflective lens for coherence and balance. The weekly plan turns selected domains, roles, and Edges into realistic action; the blotter can track daily evidence for those planned strikes. The end-of-week review helps you examine how you showed up in those roles and where the Edges helped or were lacking. A friction-based recalibration worksheet uses those insights to deliberately adjust the next week, then informs a fresh copy of the weekly plan. A monthly review consolidates evidence and recurring patterns across weeks so you can intentionally carry, change, or defer priorities for the next month.

## User-visible behavior

You can use the domain-vision landscape worksheet to:

- Choose up to three active-focus domains from adaptable examples, while treating other domains as maintenance or deferred rather than unimportant.
- Describe a longer-term vision for each selected domain and connect that vision to a meaningful role and visible evidence/action.
- Reflect on which Edges are needed, overused, or worth developing in each focus area, without turning Edges into additional domains or a required scorecard.
- Check whether visions reinforce one another or depend on neglecting another domain.
- Name one aligned next action and a cycle/review point.

You can then use a companion weekly plan, modeled on `static/worksheets/season-design/03_weekly_container.svg`, to:

- Carry forward up to three active domains and choose a small, concrete weekly action (“strike”) for each, tied to the role/vision and a relevant Edge.
- Assign an effort intention that distinguishes establishing new ground from maintaining progress, without treating the rating as personal worth.
- Give actions visible time containers and protect capacity, restoration, and bounded relationship/community connection.
- Ask what each commitment is in service to, identify a boundary/refusal/defer decision, and set a review for proof and adjustment.

Between planning and review, you can use an optional weekly blotter, inspired by `static/worksheets/weekly-blotter.svg`, to:

- Track daily evidence for the up-to-three domain/role strikes chosen on worksheet 07, without treating the tracker as a score of personal worth.
- Carry the actual marks and a selected friction moment into worksheet 08's recap and worksheet 09's recalibration; after recalibration, make a fresh copy of worksheet 07 for the next week's plan.
- Use straightforward marks (for example, done / partial / not done) and a weekly summary; no point weighting, universal target, or generic Core/Build/Sharpen/Maintain-to-domain mapping is required.

At the end of the week, you can use a role-and-Edge review modeled on `static/worksheets/season-design/04_weekly_role_card.svg` to:

- Review up to three selected roles from your focus domains using observable evidence of how you showed up, not a judgment of your worth.
- Identify which Edge(s) helped you meet a role’s responsibility, which felt lacking, and whether a strength became overused.
- Capture a pattern across the roles and choose one useful repair or next-week action.

Then use a next-week recalibration worksheet modeled on `static/worksheets/season-design/05_friction_adjustment.svg` to:

- Describe a friction point from the role review without judgment and distinguish intention from what actually happened.
- Notice your internal response, the effect on the domain/role, and the Edge that may need support, development, or tempering.
- Identify a structural cause (such as capacity, unclear action, missing time container, boundary, recovery, or support) rather than blaming character.
- Make a bounded adjustment to next week’s domain action, Edge cue, time container, support, or boundary, then name what evidence you will review.

At month-end, use a monthly review modeled on `static/worksheets/season-design/02_monthly.svg` to:

- Review your calendar and weekly evidence across up to three focus domains and their roles; distinguish what you intended from how you actually spent time and showed up.
- Notice which Edges repeatedly helped, felt lacking, or became overused across the month, without scoring yourself or trying to optimize every Edge.
- Review how capacity, restoration, relief, responsibility, and agency affected your ability to act on the vision.
- Choose what to carry forward, change, or defer for next month, including a domain/role focus, a clear outcome or milestone, a supporting rhythm/boundary, and one visible action.
- Set a monthly review point and carry the chosen priorities into the next weekly planning cycle.

## Non-goals

- Do not reproduce the full multi-hour workshop agenda or facilitator guide in either worksheet.
- Do not duplicate the vision worksheet’s full nine-Edge exposition in the weekly sheets; reuse the names and invite you to select relevant Edge(s) for your role and reflection.
- Do not turn role review into a score of your worth or prescribe one correct Edge for any role/domain.
- Do not turn recalibration into a full redesign of your life domains; adjust only the next-week plan using evidence from the prior cycle.
- Do not treat the monthly review as a mandatory restart or a full rewrite of your vision; keep what remains meaningful and revise priorities only when evidence or your season calls for it.
- Do not prescribe a fixed life-domain taxonomy, a “correct” Edge for any domain, equal effort across domains, or universal numeric goals.
- Do not modify existing worksheet files or add the new worksheet to site navigation in this task.
- Do not make the blotter a prerequisite for the weekly planning/review cycle, or duplicate worksheet 08's role/Edge interpretation and worksheet 09's structural recalibration.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **Given** the participant is beginning the exercise, **when** they open the worksheet, **then** they see a clear purpose and concise instructions distinguishing domains (where they are looking) from Edges (qualities of character they bring).
2. **Given** the participant is selecting domains, **when** they use the worksheet, **then** they can record up to three active-focus domains and see flexible examples including health/capacity, work/contribution, finances, relationships/community, family, meaning/values/growth, recreation/creativity, and home/daily life, with permission to rename/combine/replace them.
3. **Given** the participant has selected domains, **when** they complete the worksheet, **then** each of three focus areas has space for a vision, role, and visible evidence or next action.
4. **Given** the participant is reflecting through the Edges, **when** they complete the Edge section, **then** all nine are named—Direction, Craft, Alignment, Stewardship, Adaptation, Service, Legacy, Integration, Expression—and the prompts invite selection, balance, and noticing overuse without implying a score.
5. **Given** the participant has drafted visions, **when** they use the cross-domain check, **then** they can note whether the visions reinforce each other or create a tension/trade-off, and identify an integrating next action.
6. **Given** the worksheet is rendered or printed, **when** viewed at its native landscape letter ratio, **then** all content is within the 1100×850 canvas, legible, accessible via SVG title/description, and styled consistently with `static/worksheets/season-design/01_snapshot.svg` (white background, restrained black/gray palette, rounded boxes, writing lines).
7. **Given** you have completed the domain-vision worksheet, **when** you begin the weekly worksheet, **then** you can carry forward up to three focus domains and choose a weekly action for each that connects to its vision and role, with a place to identify a relevant Edge.
8. **Given** you have chosen weekly actions, **when** you complete the weekly plan, **then** you can assign effort intention (establish / maintain or equivalent), schedule visible time containers, and account for capacity/restoration and one bounded relationship/community action.
9. **Given** you review your week plan before accepting additional demands, **when** you use the alignment/refusal check, **then** you can state what your plan is in service to and record one boundary, refusal, or defer choice.
10. **Given** the week ends, **when** you use the review section, **then** you can record evidence, effort/capacity learning, and one adjustment for the next cycle. The weekly worksheet uses the same 1100×850 accessible, printable style as the source template `03_weekly_container.svg`.
11. **Given** you completed a weekly plan, **when** you review how you showed up, **then** you can record up to three focus roles, the evidence of your behavior, and whether you enacted the planned action.
12. **Given** you review each selected role, **when** you reflect on the Edges, **then** you can identify an Edge that helped, one that felt lacking or needed, and/or a strength that was overused; any self-rating is explicitly nonjudgmental and subordinate to evidence.
13. **Given** you have reviewed your roles, **when** you close the review, **then** you can name a pattern across roles and choose one repair or next-week action. The review worksheet follows the accessible 1100×850 landscape style of `04_weekly_role_card.svg`.
14. **Given** the review surfaced friction, **when** you use the recalibration worksheet, **then** you can describe what happened, compare intention with actual behavior, and note your internal response and its effect on your domain/role.
15. **Given** you identify an Edge difficulty, **when** you recalibrate, **then** you can consider which Edge needs support/development or moderation without labeling yourself as deficient.
16. **Given** you identify a repeated friction, **when** you choose an adjustment, **then** you can identify a structural cause (e.g., time, capacity, unclear action, recovery, support, or boundary) and choose a concrete next-week change to the domain action, Edge cue, container, support, or boundary.
17. **Given** you have chosen the next-week change, **when** you close the recalibration worksheet, **then** you can name evidence to track and a review point. The recalibration worksheet follows the accessible 1100×850 landscape style of `05_friction_adjustment.svg`.
18. **Given** you have completed several weekly cycles, **when** you conduct a monthly review, **then** you can compare your vision and priorities with calendar/weekly evidence across domains and roles.
19. **Given** you review the month’s role/Edge evidence, **when** you identify patterns, **then** you can name Edges that repeatedly helped, felt lacking, or were overused and reflect on capacity, restoration, relief, responsibility, and agency without grading your worth.
20. **Given** you plan the next month, **when** you complete the monthly review, **then** you can choose what to carry, change, or defer, define up to three domain/role priorities and an outcome or milestone, and name a supporting rhythm/boundary and visible action.
21. **Given** you close the monthly review, **when** you carry the plan forward, **then** you can set a review point and translate next-month priorities into the next weekly planning cycle. The monthly worksheet follows the accessible 1100×850 landscape style of `02_monthly.svg`.
22. **Given** you have planned up to three strikes on worksheet 07, **when** you use the optional blotter during the week, **then** you can record daily status against those planned domain/role strikes using clear non-scoring marks and summarize evidence without a worth judgment.
23. **Given** you finish tracking the week, **when** you use worksheets 08 and 09, **then** the blotter prompts you to bring its evidence and one friction moment into the recap/recalibration, and to transfer the bounded adjustment into a new copy of worksheet 07 for the next week.
24. **Given** you print or view the blotter, **when** using it alongside the crucible-design worksheets, **then** it is an accessible, legible 1100×850 landscape SVG with explicit title/description, printable writing space, and the same restrained visual style.
25. **Given** you use the blotter by hand, **when** recording the week's evidence, **then** the strike-name area and seven weekday cells are clearly separated, each day has a distinct usable mark area, and row/column rules do not cross labels or writing prompts.
26. **Given** you have one, two, or three active strikes, **when** you use the tracker and weekly reflection areas, **then** the layout gives useful writing room at each scale, with enough space for short evidence and one friction moment without making decorative whitespace or repeated instructions dominate.
27. **Given** you scan the page, **when** moving through planning, daily tracking, and handoff, **then** the page has a clear visual hierarchy and balanced whitespace: the daily grid is primary, the evidence/friction reflection is secondary, and the 08 → 09 → fresh 07 handoff is concise and easy to find.
28. **Given** any crucible-design worksheet refers to another sheet, **when** a participant reads its visible text or accessible title/description, **then** it identifies the companion by its title—not a sequence number or numeric shorthand.
29. **Given** any crucible-design worksheet is opened, **when** its title is read, **then** the title is context-appropriate, adjective-first, and uses the agreed sheet name: `Visionary Domains`, `Weekly Domain Plan`, `Weekly Domain Blotter`, `Weekly Role Review`, `Weekly Recalibration`, or `Monthly Domain Review`.
