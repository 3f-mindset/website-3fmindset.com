# Crucible Journal Prompts

## Goal

Create a reader-facing guided Q&A that extends the Crucible worksheets with enough structured reflection to produce a concrete working vision document. That document should be usable in later program stages to select Edges to hone, roles to promote, and weekly strikes to plan.

## User-visible behavior

Readers can move through a practical set of journal prompts aligned with Crucible's existing progression: opening foundations; Visionary Domains (including every worksheet detail and choice); Weekly Domain Plan; optional evidence/Blotter; Weekly Role Review; Weekly Recalibration; and Monthly Domain Review. Prompts elicit specific examples, desired direction, trade-offs, capacity, and observable actions, and conclude by consolidating answers into a portable working vision document.

## Non-goals

- Do not replace, alter, or duplicate the worksheets; the journal prompts prepare and enrich their use.
- Do not prescribe domains, roles, Edge selections, outcomes, cadence, or a universal vision.
- Do not convert Edge reflection, evidence, or effort into a score or judgment of worth.
- Do not turn weekly recalibration into a wholesale redesign of life direction.
- Do not require the optional blotter or imply monthly review replaces weekly work.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **Given** a reader begins the prompt set, **when** they start reflecting, **then** they understand the purpose, how to use/skip prompts, and that domain examples and Edges are optional reflection aids rather than a taxonomy or scorecard.
2. **Given** the reader explores direction, **when** they answer the opening and vision prompts, **then** they can identify what matters now, distractions/space to protect, choose up to three active domains, describe longer-term vision and desired role in each, and identify visible proof or a next action.
3. **Given** the reader has chosen focus domains, **when** they reflect on the Visionary Domains worksheet, **then** prompts cover the nine named Edges and needed/balanced/overused/developing qualities; inter-domain reinforcement and tensions including what must not be neglected; an integrating action, next action, and review point.
4. **Given** a reader prepares weekly work, **when** they answer weekly planning prompts, **then** they can choose up to three domain/role/vision-linked strikes, relevant Edge, concrete action, time container, Establish/Maintain effort, capacity, restoration, optional bounded relationship/community action, service/purpose, and boundary/refusal/defer choice.
5. **Given** a reader reviews evidence, **when** they use the prompt set, **then** they can record evidence and learning without judgment, use or skip the optional blotter, identify friction, review observable role behavior and planned-action status, notice helpful/lacking/overused Edges, patterns, and one observable next-week practice.
6. **Given** friction warrants recalibration, **when** the reader answers those prompts, **then** they can distinguish facts/intention/actual action, inner response/protective story or need, impacts, structural causes, and define one bounded next-week adjustment with Edge cue, container, boundary, support/recovery, timing, evidence, and review point.
7. **Given** the reader reaches the fourth weekly cycle, **when** they do the monthly reflection, **then** they compare direction with calendar/actions, review capacity/restoration, role/Edge patterns and agency/friction, choose up to three priorities marked Carry/Change/Defer with outcome, support/boundary, visible action, and carry them into the next weekly plan.
8. **Given** the reader has completed the Q&A, **when** they consolidate it, **then** the resulting working vision document captures focus domains, longer-term direction, roles to promote, Edge qualities to hone/balance, trade-offs and maintenance needs, evidence, boundaries/support, next actions, and review points in a format directly useful for later planning.
